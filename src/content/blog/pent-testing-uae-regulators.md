Demand for penetration testing in the UAE has grown faster than the supply of people who actually do it. Most procurement conversations I get called into reveal buyers comparing page counts, not methodology. Here is how to evaluate properly.

## Legality first

Testing without explicit written authorisation in the UAE is a criminal matter, not a contractual one. Any credible engagement begins with scope documents naming target ranges, test windows, contacts on both sides, and rules of engagement including what happens if production breaks. If a vendor treats this as paperwork to rush past, walk.

## Questions that separate engineers from report factories

1. **"Show me a finding you validated manually."** Automated scanner output dressed in your logo is a vulnerability assessment, not a pentest. Ask for the chain: reproduction steps, business impact, remediation, retest result.
2. **"How do you handle healthcare or OT environments?"** My years running healthcare IT (EHR, PACS, HIS at QAM Healthcare) taught me that timing and blast-radius awareness matter more in clinical environments than clever exploits. A tester who has never stood in a server room during clinic hours will schedule a reboot mid-dialysis.
3. **"What is your SOC handoff?"** Findings should produce detection content — SIEM rules for Splunk, QRadar or LogRhythm that catch the technique, not just the CVE. Offence feeds defence or it is theatre.
4. **"Who signs the report?"** Names and credentials (CEH, CHFI, OSCP, CISSP-track leads) belong on deliverables. In regulated audits, anonymous reports raise eyebrows.

## Compliance readiness is the actual product

Regulators and ISO 27001 auditors do not want your exploit dumps; they want risk registers, remediation roadmaps and evidence packaging. The best engagements I lead end with two artefacts: a technical annex for engineers and an executive risk narrative for the board.

Test what attackers would test. Report what auditors need to see. Fix what the business can afford, in ranked order. That loop — not the PDF — is the deliverable.
